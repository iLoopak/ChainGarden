# Art direction — a greenhouse with a keeper

Nature is the hero. Technology contains it, imperfectly. Chain Garden is a tactical game played on a living cultivation tray, with a small maintenance creature for company.

## Signature composition

The 6×6 matrix owns the screen. One compact instrument row sits above it; three specimen choices live in the narrow desktop bench or directly under the portrait tray. The title grows inside the real matrix. Remove the marketing-style instruction column during play, repeated phase/status labels and long persistent descriptions. Supporting research and collection screens use specimen inspection rather than a dashboard of paragraph cards.

The frame is opaque, warm stone-green, with asymmetric soft corners, engraved coordinates and recessed soil. Selected organisms and active routes carry light; inactive technology recedes. Forest ribs in the local background SVG remain low contrast. No remote fonts, emoji plant art, image hotlinks, RGB frames or neon instrumentation walls.

## Palette and type

| Role            | Color     | Use                                 |
| --------------- | --------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Forest          | `#102019` | greenhouse foundation               |
| Soil            | `#0c1d16` | depth and shadows                   |
| Moss            | `#1c3025` | opaque control surfaces             |
| Botanical paper | `#e1e9d4` | primary text                        |
| Living mint     | `#bce7c5` | guidance and energy                 |
| Lime            | `#c3d792` | growth/ripe tissue                  |
| Amber           | `#e5c287` | harvest decisions and reactive heat |
| Orchid          | `#c9b2dd` | copied/genetic tissue               |

System sans-serif for readable interaction, Georgia for botanical names and scores. Use sentence case. Small lab markings never carry the only explanation of a decision. Buttons, panels, instruments, navigation, disclosures and modal geometry share these tokens and materials; family accents distinguish Growth, Chain, Mutation, Reactor and Utility experiments.

## M.O.S.S. — Modular Organic Support System

An original vector maintenance drone: cracked ceramic seedpod shell, weathered copper eye gimbal and limbs, asymmetric moss crown and sprouts growing through seams. One large living eye is the expressive center. It feels like a greenhouse organism that learned maintenance, with enough mechanical detail to read as a robot at 55px.

Canonical artwork: `src/components/Moss.tsx`. Neutral uses a relaxed round aperture; curious looks sideways under a sloping lid; happy curls the eye into an arch; warning changes the aperture into an amber caution shape; surprised opens it wide. These share one silhouette and no external images. CSS float is subtle and disabled for reduced motion. There is no chatbot bubble/feed or random dialogue timer.

M.O.S.S. is prominent on the title, small beside contextual guidance and present in compact results. It never covers a plot or action. Its short lines react to real engine events; players can dismiss/mute nonessential advice. The robot is a companion, not another persistent explanatory panel.

## Plants and the matrix

Keep all eight distinct local SVG silhouettes, with root beds, veins, seed/spore nodes and small ripe cores. Three filled growth nodes and a charged marker identify maturity without relying on color. Trait marks carry the mutation color and accessible name. Soil brightness explains immediate neighbors; a mint dashed frame identifies tutorial targets; amber numbers and routes show actual forecast order and collateral is dashed warm warning color.

Plants breathe gently while the game waits. This idle art never advances growth. No clocks or timers pressure a move. On small phones, spare the anatomy from decorative frame chrome; touch targets stay near 44px where six columns permit.

## The signature reaction

Energy travels along actual source/target events. Activations brighten locally, harvest silhouettes release into a capped eight-particle burst, points rise, and the combo increases with real depth. Copying briefly reveals the copied species. Mutation transformations use actual post-event tissue snapshots. M.O.S.S. changes expression with those same events. Larger chains escalate local impact without a full-screen flash or a timer-based fake explosion.

Long playback scales to about 6.5 seconds and can be skipped. Optional procedural audio follows harvest depth. Reduced motion removes sway, travel, bursts and transition timing while preserving static routes, ordered logs and exact forecasts. Rules and rewards remain independent of presentation.
